# Physical assumptions and scope

The effective potential is Φ(r)=−μ[1+α exp(−r/λ)]/r. Its radial acceleration is −μ[1+α(1+r/λ)exp(−r/λ)]/r². Differentiating the radial factor matters: potential-based mass calibration is not acceleration-based calibration. Checks compare finite-eccentricity precession, quadrature and orbital integration.

This template is phenomenological. Pure Palatini f(R), on a regular vacuum root, does not by itself supply a propagating Yukawa scalar. A metric, hybrid or scalar–tensor action requires its own weak-field limit and observation operator. AQUAL, QUMOND, TeVeS and AeST do not automatically share predictions.

The software does not perform a global INPOP refit or infer a new graviton mass. Dynamical and optical contrasts retain the calibration assumptions stated with each result. Defining SI constants are distinguished from uncertain measured constants in `ui/physics.py` and `constants_audit/`.
